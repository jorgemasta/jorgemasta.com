# Positioning

> **Today's positioning, as of 2026-09-26. Not set in stone.** This is the
> current bet on how the site presents Jorge, and it is expected to change.
> Use it to make copy, design, and content decisions; when a decision
> contradicts it, question the document rather than silently working around it.

## Test for every decision

> **Would this make Jorge look like a thoughtful product builder with
> engineering depth and strong taste, or like another developer/AI creator on
> the internet?**

Always optimize for the former. The overall feeling: **product taste +
engineering depth + real-world shipping.**

## Core positioning

> **Product Engineer building AI-native products.**

Jorge sits at the intersection of product engineering, frontend engineering,
UX and product design, applied AI, AI evals and reliability, developer
experience, product strategy, and independent product building.

AI is a material he works with, not the subject. The subject is **building good
products**. He is not trying to become "an AI creator".

Fuller statement:

> I build digital products at the intersection of product, frontend
> engineering, and AI. I'm particularly interested in turning ambiguous ideas
> into simple products, designing AI-native experiences, and understanding the
> systems and decisions that make them reliable.

## Desired perception

After 2–3 minutes on the site, a visitor should see someone technically
strong, product-minded, opinionated but thoughtful, capable of shipping,
visually discerning, curious, ambitious, pragmatic, experienced — someone who
experiments instead of merely commenting, and who understands both
implementation and product decisions.

Not primarily: a React specialist, an AI influencer, a tutorial creator, a
freelance developer, a startup guru, a productivity influencer, a "10 AI tools"
creator, or someone chasing every model release.

## Differentiation

**Build → Observe → Form an opinion → Publish.** Not Read → Summarize →
Publish. The moat is first-hand experience: Padelful, Destilados, AI-native
experiments, eval systems, API product decisions, reliability incidents,
frontend architecture, testing AI-generated code, distribution, monetization.

## Professional narrative

**Frontend Engineer → Product Engineer → AI-native Product Builder.**

Frontend is not abandoned; it becomes one component of a broader ability:
turning ideas into products people can actually use. Emphasise increasing
scope: implementation, architecture, product decisions, UX, business
constraints, AI systems, evaluation, distribution, reliability.

## Audience, in order

1. **High-quality product companies** (Linear, Resend, Vercel, Granola,
   Raycast, small high-talent AI/product and devtool companies). Founders,
   engineering managers, product leaders, technical recruiters, senior ICs.
   Desired reaction: _"We'd like this person on our product team."_
2. **Other builders** — engineers, designers, product people, indie builders.
   Desired reaction: _"I want to follow what he's building and thinking about."_
3. **Future customers / collaborators** — secondary. The homepage must NOT look
   like a consultancy landing page.

## Brand idea and personality

> A product founder / engineer with Mediterranean warmth, technical depth, and
> strong taste. **Calm ambition.**

Ambitious without being loud, confident without arrogance, premium without
luxury for its own sake, technical without looking like developer docs.

Calm, sharp, curious, confident, thoughtful, pragmatic, warm, independent,
ambitious, slightly contrarian when justified. Avoid hype, hustle culture,
artificial authority, fake certainty, excessive enthusiasm, corporate jargon,
guru language.

## Voice

Write like someone who thinks carefully, builds real things, and doesn't need
to exaggerate: **clear + concrete + opinionated + understated.**

Prefer:

> We changed the judge. 7 of 19 batches produced different selections.

> The hard part wasn't generating the answer. It was deciding what "good" meant.

Over:

> AI evals are completely changing the future of software development.

Principles:

1. Lead with the interesting observation.
2. Use concrete numbers whenever they exist.
3. Explain decisions, not just what was built.
4. Show uncertainty when it exists ("My current hypothesis is…").
5. Avoid performative expertise.
6. Keep language simple.

## Content pillars

1. **Building AI-native products** — agent UX, LLM architectures, evals, model
   selection, reliability, criteria drift, human vs AI judgment, interfaces
   beyond chat.
2. **Product engineering** — frontend architecture, trade-offs, DX, feature
   flags, deployment, testing, AI-generated code.
3. **Building real products** — Destilados (agents, evals, filtering,
   reliability), Padelful (SEO, growth, monetization, APIs, distribution),
   other experiments.
4. **Product taste** — why some products feel simple, good defaults, removing
   UI, craft, opinionated software, design-engineering collaboration.
5. **Building a life around making things** — sparingly; human, never
   lifestyle-influencer.

### Content filter

Before publishing: Did I experience this myself? Did I build or test
something? Is the observation non-obvious? Is there a decision or trade-off?
Can I show evidence? If mostly no, don't publish.

Avoid: AI news summaries, model release recaps, generic React tutorials, tool
lists, productivity hacks, "10 things…", generic career advice, exaggerated
predictions, engagement bait. Bad: "GPT-X was released, here's everything you
need to know." Good: "I replaced our production judge with GPT-X. Here's what
actually changed."

## Visual identity

**Editorial Mediterranean Product Founder**: modern product culture +
editorial design + Mediterranean warmth, strong typography, natural materials,
restrained colour, intentional contrast.

Avoid the developer aesthetic: neon gradients, terminal windows, purple AI
gradients, matrix motifs, glowing blobs, dark SaaS landing pages, heavy
glassmorphism.

### Palette

| Role | Colour | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper | `#F4F0E8` | Backgrounds, editorial surfaces |
| Deep green | `#173F35` | Primary identity, headlines, strong accents |
| Terracotta | `#B65F3C` | Sparingly: emphasis, metadata, small details |
| Chocolate | `#3A2722` | Strong contrast, secondary dark surfaces |
| Navy | `#29313D` | Secondary dark tone |
| Charcoal | `#3E403E` | Body / dark neutral |
| Olive | `#6E7350` | Secondary natural accent |

Use more contrast than an all-neutral look: cream + deep green, cream +
chocolate, cream + navy, charcoal + warm off-white, olive + cream, terracotta
as a small accent. Don't make everything beige.

### Typography

Editorial serif for large headings, article titles, selected statements.
Modern sans for UI, body, navigation, metadata, technical content.
**Magazine × product company** — not fashion magazine, not developer docs.

### Photography

Warm natural light, Mediterranean architecture, stone, texture, natural
shadows, restrained compositions, real environments. Avoid staged corporate
portraits, stereotypical programmer photos, RGB lighting, fake laptop scenes.

## Homepage

Communicate **what Jorge builds, how Jorge thinks, what Jorge is exploring** —
not a traditional portfolio. No "My skills: React, TypeScript…" section.

Hierarchy: (1) what he's building, (2) how he thinks, (3) proof of capability,
(4) professional history. Not job title → skills → technologies → contact form.

Reference structure:

1. **Hero** — "I build thoughtful digital products." / "Product engineer
   working at the intersection of frontend, product, and AI." CTA: _See what
   I'm building →_, secondary _Read my writing →_. This leaves room to evolve
   without being branded an "AI person".
2. **What I'm building** — real products; each says what it is, why it's
   interesting, what problem Jorge is exploring. Not just logos.
3. **Notes from building** — 3–5 strong entries whose titles communicate a
   decision or discovery.
4. **Selected work** — professional experience, compact: ownership, product
   impact, systems, collaboration. Not a CV dump.
5. **About** — short and human.

### Bios

### Calls to action

Prefer: See what I'm building, Read the latest, Explore the project, Read the
case study, See how it works, Follow the experiment, View the source.
Avoid: Learn more, Discover, Unlock, Let's innovate, Transform your business.

## UX and design principles

Simple (few navigation options), fast (minimal JavaScript), opinionated,
editorial, interactive only where useful, real (real screenshots, metrics,
diagrams, experiments over abstract illustrations or AI-generated imagery).

**60% modern product website, 25% editorial publication, 15% personal
notebook.** Not a portfolio template.

Rhythm: generous whitespace, large editorial type, short copy, strong
screenshots, occasional full-width photography, diagrams, subtle UI animation.
Avoid excessive cards — not everything needs a box.
